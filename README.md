# Bangladesh Tea Market Competitive Analysis

A competitive analysis of the Bangladesh tea market, examining pricing, availability, certification, digital reach, category breadth and export status across six major brands. The analysis was conducted using Chaldal product listing data and presented as a 7-slide PowerPoint deck.

---

## Project Overview

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Dataset** | Manually collected from Chaldal, June 2026 |
| **Brands Covered** | Ispahani, Kazi & Kazi, Finlay, Seylon, Fresh,  Brooke Bond Taaza |
| **SKUs Tracked** | 55 |
| **Tools Used** | Excel (data collection & structuring), PowerPoint (presentation) |
| **Deliverable** | 6-slide competitive analysis deck |

---

## Competitive Dimensions

The analysis evaluates each brand across six dimensions:

1. **Pricing** — Average price per 100g across all SKUs
2. **Stock availability** — In-stock rate out of total listed SKUs on Chaldal
3. **Certification** — Organic and Fairtrade certification status
4. **Category breadth** — Number of distinct product categories offered
5. **Social/digital reach** — Facebook following as a proxy for domestic brand presence
6. **Export status** — International market presence

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Kazi & Kazi occupies uncontested premium territory
No competitor combines organic certification, Fairtrade status and premium pricing simultaneously. KK's average price of BDT 440/100g is more than 3× higher than the nearest mid-tier competitor (Finlay at BDT 139/100g). Within KK's own portfolio, specialty and herbal teas command BDT 525–1,307/100g, which is the highest price points in the analysis.

### 2. Teabags are priced 3–6× higher than loose tea per 100g across all brands 
The gap is driven by format and brand positioning, not input cost — loose tea prices cluster tightly at BDT 58–165/100g while teabag prices range from BDT 100–578/100g.

### 3. Finlay and Fresh show significant supply instability
Finlay has only 2 of 6 SKUs in stock (33%), and Fresh has 1 of 3 (33%). By contrast, KK maintains 79% availability and Ispahani 83%.

### 4. Ispahani dominates on social reach
Ispahani has 1.1M Facebook followers, more than 5× Kazi & Kazi's 200K. This represents a significant domestic digital gap for the premium segment.

---

## Strategic Recommendations for the Market Leader

**01 — Capture Finlay's disrupted customer base** *(Short-term · Opportunity)*
Finlay's supply instability creates an acquisition window. Health-aware mid-tier buyers paying BDT 75–150/100g are the most likely adjacent segment to trade up to KK's certified organic black tea range.

**02 — Close the domestic digital gap** *(Medium-term · Defense)*
KK's certification story, international retail presence and product quality give it stronger content material than any competitor. Closing half the gap with Ispahani through consistent social content would meaningfully expand the premium consumer funnel.

**03 — Deepen the specialty/herbal moat** *(Long-term · Growth)*
KK's specialty teas face zero certified organic competition domestically. Increasing SKU depth and in-stock reliability in this category protects the highest-margin range while competitors have no credible path to enter on certification.

---

## Methodology

Data was collected manually from Chaldal product listings in June 2026. Each SKU was recorded with brand, product name, price, weight, price per 100g, in-stock status, category and certification. Social following figures were collected from publicly available Facebook pages. The dataset covers the online retail channel only; offline retail dynamics may differ.

No statistical modeling was applied. This is a descriptive competitive analysis intended to identify positioning gaps and strategic opportunities.

---

## Limitations

- Covers online retail (Chaldal) only 
- Social following is a proxy for digital reach, not a direct measure of brand awareness or sales
- Category breadth was approximated by SKU count
- Single point-in-time snapshot and thus pricing and availability can fluctuate
